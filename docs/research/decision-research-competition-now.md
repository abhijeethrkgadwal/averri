# Competitive Intelligence: "Warrant" (contract/impact layer for agent-written TypeScript backends) — October 2026

**Bottom line.** The *review* layer Warrant wants to sell into is now one of the most crowded, best-funded categories in developer tools (CodeRabbit $1.5B, Cursor+Graphite, Greptile, Qodo $120M raised, plus first-party review from Anthropic, OpenAI, GitHub, and Cognition). The *contract* layer it wants to build on is effectively empty: nobody ships declared, compiler-verified preconditions/postconditions/policies for application code with impact analysis on PRs. The demand signal for "agents ship code that compiles but is missing auth / RLS / idempotency" is real and well-documented in 2026; the demand signal for "developers want to write contracts to fix it" is an inference, and the two best-funded attempts at adjacent declarative layers (Oso, Wasp) ended in a small acquisition and a DSL retreat in the last five months.

## (a) Contracts, invariants, and policy for backends

**Strongest: Prisma (Prisma 8 / "Prisma Next").** In Early Access since May 2026 with Postgres GA targeted June–July; explicitly framed as "write your contract, prompt your agent": the agent edits the schema contract, the framework plans migrations, and the quickstart installs project-level agent skills ([prisma.io](https://www.prisma.io/blog/prisma-next-early-access-write-your-contract-prompt-your-agent-ship-your-app), [roadmap](https://www.prisma.io/blog/prisma-next-roadmap)). Their "contract" is the data schema only; nothing about actions, policies, or effects. **Convex** raised a $57M Series B (Aug 2026, Insight; $110.5M total), sells "backend for agent-written software," and in October shipped an AI Gateway, SCIM, and schema-change warnings ([unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/convex-raises-57m-series-b-to-build-the-backend-for-agent-written-software/), [changelog](https://ship.convex.dev/changelog/convex-1-35-1)). Hosted runtime, no declared contracts. **Encore** (~$6.8M seed) now publishes a stream of SEO benchmarks: Encore 36/36 on a production-readiness rubric at $2.58/run vs NestJS 30/36 at $5.95, with the key finding that "on four of five frameworks the agent built the laziest solution that would still pass the tests" ([encore.dev](https://encore.dev/blog/ai-benchmark)). That finding is Warrant's thesis, published by a competitor who sells a framework instead. **ZenStack v3** rewrote on Kysely with policy as a plugin, and its 2026 content pivoted to "your stack is the guardrail for coding agents" plus ZenStack Studio (database-to-MCP with auth) ([zenstack.dev](https://zenstack.dev/blog), [docs](https://zenstack.dev/docs/migrate-v2)). Closest philosophical neighbor; row-level, ORM-bound. **Wasp** formally killed its DSL for a TypeScript spec on June 15, 2026 after ~$5M and five years ([wasp.sh](https://wasp.sh/blog/2026/06/15/wasp-typescript-spec)). **Keel** and **Nitric** produced no 2026 news of note ([Keel profile](https://simplify.jobs/c/Keel)). **Supabase** added agent skills whose own text says "agents often skip RLS," weekly advisor emails, and at Select 2026 (Oct 2) scoped tokens and MCP elicitations before paid actions ([supabase.com](https://supabase.com/blog/supabase-agent-skills), [security retro](https://supabase.com/blog/supabase-security-2025-retro)). **Better Auth** is still on a $5M seed ([better-auth.com](https://www.better-auth.com/blog/seed-round)).

**Authorization vendors:** **Oso** launched "Oso for Coding Agents" (Jan 27, 2026) and was then acquired by Tiny on Oct 6, 2026, a holding-company exit for the best-funded app-authz startup (~$26M raised) ([osohq.com/press](https://osohq.com/press)). Styra's commercial OPA offering was sunset after Apple hired the OPA maintainers (per Oso's blog). **Cedar** became the policy engine for Bedrock AgentCore (GA Mar 3, 2026) and Verified Permissions aligned with Cedar in May ([AWS docs](https://docs.amazonaws.cn/en_us/verifiedpermissions/latest/userguide/terminology-differences-avp-cedar.html)). **Permit.io** last raised $8M in Feb 2024 ([businesswire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240213359627/en/Permit.io-Raises-%248-Million-to-Free-Engineers-to-Write-Code-Not-Policies)). Authz-as-a-service has been a hard business; the energy has moved to "authorize the agent," not "declare the app's policy."

**Gap left for Warrant:** action-level `requires/ensures/policy` as verified graph facts, auth-matrix and pitfall test generation, contract-weakening detection. Nobody in (a) does any of it. Prisma 8 owns the word "contract" for the data layer, which is a naming collision Warrant will fight forever.

## (b) Agent-written-code governance and review

This is where the money went in 2026. **CodeRabbit**: $143M Series C at $1.5B (Aug 2026), ~$40M ARR est., $24/$48 per PR-author/month, plus code-graph analysis, LanceDB semantic search, natural-language **pre-merge checks** as quality gates, and Learnings ([scalevp](https://www.scalevp.com/press/coderabbit-raises--60m--valuing-the-2-year-old-ai-code-review-startup-at--550m), [sacra](https://sacra.com/c/coderabbit/), [docs](https://docs.coderabbit.ai/pr-reviews/pre-merge-checks.md)). **Cursor** ($50B valuation, ~$2B ARR) owns **Graphite** (acq. Dec 2025, Diamond folded into Graphite Agent, $40/user Team tier) and shipped Bugbot usage pricing (~$1–1.50/run), effort levels, `/review`, and **Security Review** agents for Teams/Enterprise (beta Apr 30, Sept update) ([siliconangle](https://siliconangle.com/2025/12/19/cursor-acquires-ai-code-review-startup-graphite/), [aicatchup](https://aicatchup.com/news/cursor-bugbot-3x-faster-review-command-incremental-review), [skillselion](https://skillselion.com/brief/cursor/cursor-weekly-sdk-launch-security-agents-and-marketplace-overhaul)). **Greptile**: $25M Series A (Benchmark), moved to $1/credit with no seat fee, launched **TREX** (June 2026): generates and runs tests per PR in a sandbox, 3 credits/run ([greptile.com/blog/trex](https://www.greptile.com/blog/trex), [changelog](https://greptile.com/changelog)). **Qodo**: $70M Series B (Mar 2026, $120M total), pooled-credit pricing, enterprise logos (Walmart, NVIDIA, Intuit) ([dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/127108-qodo-raises-70m-to-govern-the-ai-code-flood-as-enterprises-lose-trust-in/)). **First-party review**: GitHub Copilot code review moved to an agentic architecture in March, reads custom instructions and MCP/skills, but *only comments, never blocks* and caps instruction files at 4,000 chars ([github.blog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-03-05-copilot-code-review-now-runs-on-an-agentic-architecture/), [docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/copilot-on-github/use-copilot-agents/copilot-code-review)). Anthropic's **Code Review** (research preview Mar 9, Team/Enterprise, $15–25/PR) and **Claude Security** (public beta May) ([claude.com](https://claude.com/resources/articles/code-review), [support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14233555-set-up-code-review-for-claude-code), [pulse2](https://pulse2.com/anthropic-launches-claude-security-in-public-beta-for-enterprise-customers)). OpenAI **Codex review** flags only P0/P1 and reads AGENTS.md ([openai docs](https://developers.openai.com/codex/integrations/github)). **Devin Review** (Jan 2026) plus autofix ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/cognition-ai-company-overview/)); Cognition at $48B, ~$900M run-rate ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/cognition-raises-over-2-billion-at-48-billion-valuation-as-run-rate-revenue-nears-900-million/)). Security side: **Socket** $60M at $1B (May), **Snyk Evo** 60% of new deal volume, **Semgrep Multimodal** with MCP built into the binary, auth-flaw detection ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/socket-60-million-series-c-raised-at-1-billion-valuation-to-help-enterprises-secure-ai-generated-code/), [snyk.io](https://snyk.io/news/agentic-ai-security-moves-into-production/), [businesswire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260319711078/en/Semgrep-Launches-Multimodal-Combining-AI-Reasoning-With-Rule-Based-Analysis-for-Detection-Triage-and-Remediation)). **Sourcery** is a Python-centric $10–12/seat niche.

**Gap:** every reviewer infers intent from the diff plus prose rules; none consumes a declared contract, so none can say "this PR weakened `requires.notAlreadyMember`" with certainty. CodeRabbit's natural-language pre-merge checks and Greptile's TREX are the nearest approximations and will keep getting closer.

Commoditized. **Augment Context Engine MCP** GA Feb 6, 2026, 40–70 credits/query, Business at $100/mo flat for 50 seats ([augmentcode.com](https://www.augmentcode.com/blog/context-engine-mcp-now-live), [docs](https://docs.augmentcode.com/context-services/mcp/overview.md)). **CodeGraph** (colbymchenry) launched Jan 2026, ~66K stars by August, local SQLite, free ([buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/codebase-knowledge-graph-ast-coding-agents-2026)). **Sourcegraph** split from Amp (Dec 2025), now $16K/yr enterprise-only; Amp relaunched May 2026 ([getunblocked](https://getunblocked.com/blog/sourcegraph-alternatives/)). **Claude Code** has built-in LSP and Auto Memory on by default since Feb 2026 ([circleci](https://circleci.com/blog/claude-code-lsp/), [memoryplugin](https://blog.memoryplugin.com/claude-code-memory/)); **Codex** got built-in memory Apr 16 ([openai](https://openai.com/index/codex-for-almost-everything/)); **Antigravity 2.0** (May 19) has a knowledge base and artifacts but no enterprise support and no releases since August ([wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Antigravity)). **Gap:** none carries contracts, effects, or ownership as verified facts. But the strategy document already concedes navigation is unsellable; (c) is a free arm in the benchmark, not a market.

## (d) Spec-driven development

**Spec Kit** hit 1.0 in August with 38 agent integrations, 157 extensions, 100K+ stars ([spec-kit history](https://github.github.com/spec-kit/history.html)). **Kiro** is GA, credit-priced $20–200/mo, and ships **property-based tests derived from specs** plus planned automated reasoning to check spec consistency ([kiro.dev](https://kiro.dev/blog/general-availability/), [thenewstack](https://thenewstack.io/aws-kiro-brings-automated-reasoning-to-agentic-development/)). That is the closest thing to "generated tests from declared intent" shipped by a hyperscaler, albeit from Markdown, not a typed contract language. **Tessl** repositioned its $125M spec bet into a skills registry (Jan 2026) ([codemyspec](https://codemyspec.com/blog/tessl-review)). **Gap:** specs are prose interpreted by an LLM, unenforced after generation. Kiro's PBT is the exception and the warning.

## (e) AI-native languages and representations

**Vercel Labs Zero** (May 2026, Apache-2, pre-1.0): systems language with `zero check --json`, stable error codes, repair metadata, explicit effects in signatures ([mer.vin](https://mer.vin/2026/05/zero-language-vercel-labs-agent-first-diagnostics-and-explicit-effects/), [noqta](https://noqta.tn/en/news/vercel-labs-zero-programming-language-ai-agents-2026)). It validates Warrant's diagnostics design and is in a different domain. **Jac/Jaseci** v0.32 added ownership/borrow checking; **Sigil** compiles SKILL.md to enforced programs (56%→86% step compliance) ([jaseci digest](https://buttondown.com/jaseci-newsletter/archive/jaseci-digest-05-news-from-the-jaseci-open-source/)). **MoonBit** still beta-preview, no adoption data. The research direction that matters is **"Steerability via constraints"** (ICML 2026 DL4Code workshop): enforced substrate plus a 200-LoC `docs` CLI lifted backdoor recall from 54.5% to 90.9% at lower token cost than scaffolding ([arxiv 2607.02389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02389)). No Anthropic/OpenAI/Google research on agent-optimized code IRs surfaced. **Gap:** nobody is doing contracts for TypeScript backends as a representation; the only direct hit is **Sensei** (Globulario), a tiny Windows-installer tool exposing invariants as a queryable graph plus CI gate ([wingetly](https://wingetly.io/apps/globulario/sensei)).

## Who ships Warrant's core natively in 12–24 months

1. **CodeRabbit** is most likely. It already has the three ingredients: a code graph for impact, natural-language pre-merge checks that act as gates, and a $143M war chest. Turning "Undocumented Breaking Changes" into "Policy removed on payment route" is a product decision, not a research project. What it lacks is a declaration layer in the repo, and it has no incentive to ask developers to write one.
2. **Cursor/Graphite** second: Security Review agents plus Bugbot plus Graphite's merge-queue gates equal policy-as-code on PRs the moment they add a rules file richer than `.cursorrules`.
3. **Kiro/AWS** is the dark horse for the *test-generation* half: PBT from specs plus automated reasoning is the same promise with Markdown input.
4. **Prisma** will extend "contract" upward (policies on the schema, agent-safe actions) because it already named the concept and ships agent skills.
Anthropic/GitHub/OpenAI will not build a cross-vendor contract language; they will build better inference from diffs, which erodes the *perceived* need rather than copying the design.

## Is the contracts gap real demand or analyst inference?

**The failure mode is real and measured.** 170/1,645 Lovable showcase apps had RLS disabled (CVE-2025-48757); a Feb 2026 Lovable Discover app leaked ~18.7K user records via inverted access logic; Moltbook exposed 1.5M API tokens in 72 hours because RLS was never configured ([rankiteo](https://blog.rankiteo.com/mussup1772216763-supabase-lovable-vulnerability-february-2026/), [kunalganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/vibe-code-security-nightmares.md)). CSA's April 2026 note reports 45–70% of AI-generated samples failing security tests, with authorization and cross-service checks as the worst class ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-codegen-vulnerability-debt-2026)); Georgia Tech counted 74 AI-linked CVEs through March. Sygnia's July pentest found a vibe-coded token issuer that never validated entitlement ([lasvegassun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/jul/28/sygnia-penetration-test-reveals-critical-vibe-code/)). GitLab's 1,528-respondent report: 92% have an AI-code governance challenge, 34% of incident-hit orgs could not tell if AI code contributed ([securitybrief](https://securitybrief.co.uk/story/gitlab-warns-of-ai-code-governance-gap-in-new-report)). Agents weakening tests is documented by SWE-Atlas and widely reported anecdotally ([arxiv 2605.08366](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08366), [producthunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/vibecoding/how-do-you-stop-ai-coding-agents-from-weakening-tests-to-make-them-pass)). Prose rules being ignored is a filed Claude Code issue, and the practitioner consensus is "hooks for must-rules, prose for guidance" ([claudeissues 70420](https://claudeissues.com/issue/70420-claude-md-rules-arent-reliably-followed-pretooluse-hooks-are-a-partial-hand-roll), [bitbytebit](https://bitbytebit.substack.com/p/agent-hooks-deterministic-guardrails)).

**The remedy is inference.** Nobody is asking for a contract library. The market's answer to every incident above has been: platform defaults (Supabase advisors, Prisma removing `reset`), security scanners (Semgrep auth-flaw detection, Claude Security), and smarter diff review. The Encore benchmark result ("laziest solution that passes") and Kiro's PBT are the only mainstream signals that people want *specifications that generate tests*, and both chose framework/Markdown over a contract language. The "write contracts" ask appears only in blog essays (CONTRACT.md, ADR-with-hooks) and one workshop paper.

## Crowded, nascent, or empty?

The review/governance layer is **crowded** and consolidating, with first-party reviewers bundled at zero marginal cost. The declared-contract layer is **empty**, and here empty is mostly a bad sign: Oso (declarative authz, Sequoia-backed) exited to a holding company this month; Wasp retreated from its declarative layer in June; Keel went vertical; Styra folded. Every adjacent attempt to get developers to declare semantics outside their code has underperformed. The one honest reading in Warrant's favor is that the *reason* those failed (humans would not do the declaring) is changing, because agents can draft contracts and `warrant suggest` is exactly that. That is a thesis worth $200K to test, not $650K to assume.

## Top 5 threats, ranked

1. **CodeRabbit** adds contract-aware pre-merge checks and impact from its existing code graph, with $143M to do it and 1.5B valuation pressure to expand the ARPU; Warrant's Team tier becomes a checkbox.
2. **First-party review bundling** (Anthropic Code Review, Copilot agentic review, Codex, Devin Review) sets the price of "a PR bot" at zero and keeps improving at inferring intent, shrinking the perceived need for declarations.
3. **Cursor/Graphite Security Review + merge gates** ship policy-as-code for PRs inside the editor 40% of the target buyers already pay for.
4. **Prisma 8** owns the word "contract" and the schema, and extends into policies and agent-safe actions, leaving Warrant explaining why it is not "Prisma's contract."
5. **Kiro-style spec-derived property tests** go cross-IDE (Spec Kit extension or Copilot feature), delivering 70% of the generated-test value from Markdown with no new library to learn.
