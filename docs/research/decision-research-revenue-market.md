## 2. Market sizing

**AI code review / agent-written code governance.** Pure-play PR-review tools: roughly $180M (2025) to ~$420M ARR (2026) by one analyst estimate, $400-600M by another; the broad definition including quality platforms and scanners is $2-3B ([ideaplan](https://www.ideaplan.io/blog/ai-code-review-tools-market-share-2026), [dev.to](https://dev.to/rahulxsingh/the-state-of-ai-code-review-in-2026-trends-tools-and-whats-next-2gfh)). Secondary reporting attributes ~$9.8-11B for "AI coding agents" in 2026 to Gartner, and Gartner expects AI-assisted development cost per developer to exceed a developer's salary by 2028 ([FinChannel](https://finchannel.com/ai-coding-could-cost-more-than-hiring-developers-by-2028-gartner-says/131828/american-business-trends/markets/2026/06/)). Adoption signals: 1.3M repos with an AI review integration (Octoverse 2025) and 30% of 1,000+-dev enterprises had deployed one by end-2025 ([aicodereview.cc](https://aicodereview.cc/blog/state-of-ai-code-review-2026)). No analyst publishes a line item for "governance of agent-written code"; the sources that address buyers describe two distinct purchasers: an engineering manager buying review-noise reduction, and a security/platform lead buying a merge-blocking gate with an audit trail ([Unblocked](https://getunblocked.com/blog/best-ai-code-review-tools/)). Veracode's 2026 report (56% of AI-generated code passes security checks) is what pulls AppSec budget in. GitHub is building its own "agent governance control plane" (Agent HQ) in-house ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/github-copilot-surges-as-agent-hq-targets-ai-ecosystem)), and Snyk shipped Evo for agent/MCP vetting.

Hype TAM: $2-3B now, $10B+ if you count agents. Realistic serviceable market for Warrant: TypeScript backends on Prisma + Hono/Express in teams of 30-300 engineers with a named AI-enablement owner. That is a few thousand companies globally; at $20/dev × ~50 devs × 12 months = $12K/yr per Team account, 100 accounts is $1.2M. The enterprise tier is where the money is, and it competes for the same AppSec/platform budget as Snyk, Semgrep, Socket, GitHub Advanced Security, and now CodeRabbit (Datadog-backed).

**Backend frameworks / BaaS.** Estimates: $12.3B (2025) to $15.3B (2026) to $36.8B (2030) at ~25% CAGR on the broad definition; narrower mobile-BaaS $8.4B to $16.7B by 2032 at ~10% ([TBRC](https://thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/backend-as-a-service-market-report), [MarkNtel](https://www.marknteladvisors.com/query/request-sample/cloud-mobile-baas-market.html)). Growth is being captured by hosted Postgres and agent-provisioned infra (Supabase: AI tools create the majority of new databases; Claude Code is its largest new-usage source in 2026). Warrant does not host anything in V0, so it participates in this market only as a complement.

## 3. How open-core devtools convert in 2026

- **Conversion rates.** Commonly repeated benchmarks: 1-5% free-to-paid for broad developer tools, 5-10% for specialized infrastructure, 15-20% only for enterprise-targeted products; developer tools skew to the low end, 1-3% ([Monetizely citing OpenView/Bessemer](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/whats-the-right-ratio-of-free-to-paid-users-in-developer-saas)). No 2025-26 primary benchmark exists; Sentry's ~70% self-serve revenue mix and PostHog's 20% MoM paid growth are the closest disclosures ([Pocus](https://www.pocus.com/blog/posthog-case-study)). Applied to the plan's M6 target of 100 weekly-active repos, 2-5% is 2-5 paying accounts, not 20.
- **Seat prices.** Category average ~$20/user/mo; CodeRabbit $24-72, Graphite $20-40, Greptile $30 + credits, Cody ~$59, with a visible shift to usage/credit pricing as agent PR volume rises ([Costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-code-review/)). $20/dev is at the floor; fine for wedge, thin for a two-person company.
- **Time to enterprise deal.** Median B2B SaaS cycle 84 days; $100K+ ACV runs 90-180+ days; security review, DDQ, and redlines add 4-10 weeks; one AppSec buyer calls seven months "fast" ([Arcade](https://www.arcade.software/post/enterprise-sales-cycle), [Cyberbase](https://cyberbase.ai/blog/enterprise-saas-deal-acceleration), [AppSec Santa](https://appsecsanta.com/newsletter/2026-w24)). The plan's "convert design partners at M10-12" should be read as M14-18.
- **Procurement obstacles.** SOC 2 Type I audit $7.5-15K and 8-12 weeks; Type II $30-100K and 4-6 months, and savvy buyers want a 6-month observation window; first-year all-in $25-60K with automation; buyers now also ask for a pen test and a DPA ([Drata](https://drata.com/learn/soc-2/cost), [Scrut](https://www.scrut.io/soc-2/cost-of-soc-2-audit), [Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/soc2-for-seed-startups-enterprise-deals-2026)). The plan's "SOC2 starts month 8" means a Type II report around month 14-16, which is when $50K+ deals can actually clear. The in-CI design (only the report leaves the VPC) is the right mitigation; it is also what GitHub, Snyk and CodeRabbit self-hosted already offer.
- **What a 2-person team actually reaches.** Reference points: CodeRabbit (2 founders, then a VC-funded team) took ~18 months to $1M; Inngest (a16z-backed) ~3 years to $1M; Plausible (4 people, bootstrapped) ~2 years, with 324 days from first subscriber to $400 MRR; Flagsmith >$1M only with an agency subsidizing it ([Flagsmith](https://flagsmith.com/blog/developer-led-podcast-bootstrapping-a-commerical-open-source-company)). None of these also had a founder splitting time with a second startup.

## What revenue a 2-person team should realistically expect here

Frankly: the plan's $150-250K ARR at M12 and $400-700K at M18 is a top-decile outcome for a two-person open-core devtool, and the comparables do not support it as a base case. The only company in this category that moved that fast, CodeRabbit, had a general-purpose product any GitHub repo could install in two minutes, a $16M Series A by month ~18, and still took roughly 18 months to reach $1M. Warrant's wedge requires Prisma + Hono/Express + Zod + a willingness to declare contracts, which cuts the funnel to a small fraction of CodeRabbit's, and the $20/dev PR bot is priced at the category floor against four funded incumbents plus GitHub's native review. A realistic base case with two people and a founder who is also launching another company: $0-60K ARR at M12 (a few Team accounts and one paid pilot), $100-250K at M18 if the M6 benchmark is genuinely strong and one design partner converts, and $300K-1M at M36 if the enterprise gate finds an AppSec budget owner and SOC 2 Type II is in hand by M16. The upside case ($1-2M by M36) requires the benchmark to land, a $1.5-2.5M seed at M12-15, and the founder full-time; the downside case, which the comparables (Encore, Wasp, Tessl, ZenStack, Prisma's ORM) make the modal one for declarative-backend ideas, is a well-liked OSS project with under $100K ARR after three years. The exits that have actually happened in 2025-26 paid for installed base and teams, not benchmarks, so the realistic "good" outcome for a two-person version is an acqui-hire in the low tens of millions by a coding-agent or infra vendor, not a standalone company. If the founder cannot commit full-time by M3, the evidence says pass or shrink it to the lint-bot pivot the strategy document already names as the fallback.
